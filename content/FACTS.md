## Deadlines
- **CCP §335.1** — personal injury and wrongful death: 2 years. (Wrongful death runs from the date of death.)
- **CCP §340.5** — medical negligence: 3 years from injury or 1 year from discovery, *whichever comes first*. Tolled for fraud, intentional concealment, or a foreign body left in the patient. Minors: 3 years from the wrongful act; a child under 6 has until 3 years or the 8th birthday, whichever is longer.
- **CCP §364** — at least 90 days' notice of intent before suing a health care provider; if served within the last 90 days of the limitations period, the deadline extends 90 days from service.
- **CCP §352** — the deadline is paused while the injured person is a minor or lacks legal capacity. It does **not** apply to claims that require a government claim.
- **Gov. Code §911.2** — government claim for death, injury to a person, or damage to personal property (e.g., a vehicle) or growing crops: within **6 months** of accrual. Other claims: 1 year.
- **Gov. Code §911.4** — application to file a late government claim: within a reasonable time, not more than 1 year after accrual.
- **Gov. Code §911.6** — the public entity **must** grant a late-claim application (filed within §911.4's one year) if, among other grounds, the injured person was a minor for the entire 6-month claim period, or was physically or mentally incapacitated for that entire period and failed to file because of it. (Partial periods: the application must come within 6 months after turning 18 / regaining capacity, or 1 year after accrual, whichever is first.) So minors don't get the usual pause (§352), but do get this late-claim route — say so rather than "children get no extra time".
- **Gov. Code §945.6** — after a government claim is rejected in writing: sue within 6 months of the notice being delivered/mailed; if no written notice, within 2 years of accrual.

## Wrongful death and survival
- **CCP §377.60** — who can sue for wrongful death: (a) surviving spouse, domestic partner, children, issue of deceased children; if none, those who would inherit by intestate succession. (b) If dependent on the decedent: putative spouse, children of a putative spouse, stepchildren, parents. (c) A minor who lived in the decedent's household for the previous 180 days and depended on the decedent for half or more of their support.
- **CCP §377.30** — survival action is brought by the decedent's personal representative or, if none, successor in interest.
- **CCP §377.34** — survival damages are the losses the decedent sustained before death (e.g., medical bills, lost earnings) and can include punitive damages. Pain, suffering, or disfigurement were recoverable only in cases filed on or after Jan 1, 2022 and **before Jan 1, 2026** (or granted trial preference before 2022). For cases filed now, they generally are not. Say: "a temporary rule allowed it for cases filed 2022–2025."
- General (no citation): wrongful death damages cover the family's losses — financial support, household services, gifts/benefits, funeral and burial costs, and loss of love, companionship, comfort, care, assistance, protection, affection, society, moral support, and (for children) training and guidance. Not recoverable in the wrongful death claim: the family's grief/sorrow, and punitive damages. All heirs generally bring **one** wrongful death action together.

## Fault and damages
- General (no citation): California uses **pure comparative fault** — an injured person can recover even if partly at fault; the award is reduced by their percentage of fault.
- **Civ. Code §1431.2** (Proposition 51) — each defendant pays non-economic damages only in proportion to its own percentage of fault (economic damages remain joint).
- **Civ. Code §3333.4** (Proposition 213) — no recovery of non-economic damages (pain and suffering) for: a driver convicted of DUI for the accident; an owner of an uninsured vehicle involved in the accident; an operator who can't establish financial responsibility. Exception: an uninsured owner hit by a driver who was DUI can still recover non-economic damages.
- **Civ. Code §3333.2** (MICRA, as amended 2022) — caps on non-economic damages against health care providers. Started 2023 at $350,000 (injury) / $500,000 (death); rise each Jan 1 by $40,000 / $50,000 until $750,000 / $1,000,000 (2033), then +2%/year from 2034. **2026 figures: $470,000 (injury) and $650,000 (death).** Separate caps apply to (1) health care providers, (2) health care institutions, and (3) unaffiliated providers/institutions — up to three caps per case. Economic damages (medical bills, lost earnings) are not capped.

## Vehicles
- **Veh. Code §16056** — minimum auto liability insurance since Jan 1, 2025: $30,000 per person / $60,000 per accident bodily injury / $15,000 property damage. (Scheduled to rise again in 2035.)
- **Veh. Code §16000** — drivers must report a crash to the DMV within 10 days if anyone is injured or killed, or property damage exceeds $1,000 (form SR-1).
- **Pub. Util. Code §5433** (TNC/rideshare — Uber, Lyft) — app on, no ride accepted: at least $50,000 per person / $100,000 per incident / $30,000 property. From accepting a ride until the passenger exits: $1,000,000 liability. Uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage while a passenger is in the car: $60,000 per person / $300,000 per incident (lowered by SB 371, effective Jan 1, 2026).
- **Veh. Code §22406** — trucks with 3+ axles and vehicles towing: max 55 mph.
- Federal hours-of-service rules (49 C.F.R. Part 395, cite only as "federal hours-of-service rules"): property-carrying drivers may drive at most 11 hours, within a 14-hour on-duty window, after 10 hours off. Trucks keep electronic logging device (ELD) data and engine/"black box" data — preserve quickly.
- **Veh. Code §27803** — all motorcycle drivers and passengers must wear a safety helmet meeting state standards.
- **Veh. Code §21658.1** — defines lane splitting (two-wheeled motorcycle between rows of stopped or moving vehicles in the same lane); CHP may publish safety guidelines. Lane splitting is legal in California; it is not automatically negligence.
- **Veh. Code §21760** — drivers must leave at least 3 feet when passing a bicyclist in the same direction.
- **Veh. Code §21955** (Freedom to Walk Act, 2023) — police may not stop a pedestrian for crossing outside a crosswalk unless a reasonably careful person would see an immediate danger of collision.
- General (no citation): pedestrians have the right of way in marked and unmarked crosswalks at intersections; drivers must exercise due care.

## Other practice areas
- **Civ. Code §3342** — a dog owner is liable for damages when the dog bites someone in a public place or lawfully in a private place — regardless of prior viciousness or the owner's knowledge (strict liability). Applies to bites; other dog injuries (knock-downs) are negligence claims.
- **Labor Code §3602** — workers' compensation is generally the exclusive remedy against an employer. Exceptions: willful physical assault by the employer; employer's fraudulent concealment of an injury that aggravates it; a defective product the employer manufactured and sold to a third party that was then provided to the employee. Third parties (contractors, equipment makers, property owners, other drivers) can be sued.
- **Welf. & Inst. Code §15657** (Elder Abuse Act) — if physical abuse or neglect with recklessness, oppression, fraud, or malice is proven by clear and convincing evidence, the court must award attorney's fees and costs, and the victim's pain and suffering can be recovered even after death (subject to limits). Applies to elders (65+) and dependent adults.
- **Gov. Code §835** — a public entity is liable for a dangerous condition of its property if the condition created a foreseeable risk of the injury, it caused the injury, and either an employee created it or the entity had notice in time to fix it.
- General (no citation): property owners and occupiers must use reasonable care to keep property safe (premises liability). Product liability: manufacturers and sellers are strictly liable for manufacturing defects, design defects, and inadequate warnings.

## For guides (resources) — verified 2026-10-02
- **Bus. & Prof. Code §6147** — a contingency fee contract must be in writing, signed by attorney and client, with a duplicate copy given to the client when signed. It must state: the fee rate; how costs and disbursements affect the fee and the client's recovery; whether the client may owe for related matters not covered by the contract; and (except in medical negligence cases) that the fee is not set by law and is negotiable. If not followed, the contract is voidable by the client and the attorney gets only a reasonable fee.
- **Ins. Code §11580.2** — auto policies must include uninsured motorist (UM) bodily injury coverage unless the insured deletes or lowers it by a written agreement. A hit-and-run vehicle counts as uninsured if the accident was reported to police within **24 hours** (among other conditions). Fault and damages in a UM claim are decided by agreement with your insurer or, if you disagree, by **arbitration**. Within **two years** of the accident, one of these must happen: suit filed against the uninsured motorist, an agreement on the amount, or arbitration formally started.
- **CCP §36** — trial preference: a party over 70 with a substantial interest whose health makes it necessary; a party under 14 in a personal injury or wrongful death case (unless no substantial interest). Once granted, trial must be set within **120 days**.
- **Civ. Code §3045.1** — a licensed hospital has a lien on damages the patient (or heirs/personal representative) recovers from the person who caused the injury, for reasonable and necessary charges for treatment. Not for injuries covered by workers' compensation.
- **Civ. Code §3045.4** — a hospital lien can't exceed **50%** of the money due under the judgment, compromise, or settlement after paying any prior liens.
- **Prob. Code §3500** — a parent may settle (compromise) a minor's injury claim, but it is valid only after **court approval** on a petition to the superior court.
- **Veh. Code §20012** — traffic accident reports may be released to people with a proper interest: drivers involved, injured persons, vehicle/property owners, parents of a minor driver, authorized representatives (e.g., attorneys), and others who may face liability. Fees may not exceed the actual cost of the copy.
- **CCP §377.61** — in a wrongful death action, damages are what is "just" under all the circumstances (not including survival damages); **the court determines each heir's share** of the award.
