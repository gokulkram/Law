## Stated generally, not on the fact sheet — verify
**Wrongful death pages**
- Putative spouse = someone who believed in good faith they were validly married; treatment of an heir who refuses to join (one-action rule).
- Survival action: personal representative = executor/court-appointed administrator; successor in interest; recovery runs through the estate; examples of conduct supporting punitive damages.
- Workers' comp pays without proof of fault and is more limited than a wrongful death recovery; comp claim and third-party claim can proceed at the same time.
- Civil claims use a lower standard of proof than criminal cases; UM/UIM on the decedent's own policy; a claim may be possible when the at-fault driver also died.
- Commercial carriers "often carry more coverage than a private driver."
- Left out: effect of decedent's comparative fault / Prop 213 on heirs' claims; whether a surviving child's minority tolls the wrongful death deadline; intestate succession order; survival action deadline; when §340.5 runs after a death; which year's MICRA cap applies and whether it's shared among heirs; whether a criminal case extends deadlines; workers' comp death benefit details and lien/credit.

**Vehicle pages**
- Employers are generally responsible for employee drivers' negligence on the job.
- Uber/Lyft treat drivers as independent contractors; claims usually go through the required insurance; trucking companies may argue the same.
- Not wearing a helmet doesn't bar a claim; under comparative fault it generally affects only injuries a helmet would have prevented.
- A pedestrian's or cyclist's own auto UM/UIM and MedPay "may apply."
- Duty to check before opening a car door into traffic (no citation given).
- When the rideshare app is off, the driver's personal policy normally applies.

**Serious injury and dog bite pages**
- Dog bites: comparative fault generally *reduces* rather than bars a strict-liability award; a caretaker or landlord who knew of a dangerous dog "may share responsibility"; homeowner's/renter's insurance "often covers dog bites."
- Left out: family-provided care as damages; guardians ad litem/conservators for incapacitated adults; Medicare/Medi-Cal/health-insurer liens, special needs trusts, structured settlements; §3342 police/military dog exception; veterinarian/groomer assumption of risk.

**Other practice areas**
- Nursing home: the 2-year deadline (§335.1) "generally" applies; §340.5 "may apply instead" if the claim is mainly about medical care — confirm which governs.
- Product liability reaches component makers and distributors; no recall needed to bring a claim.
- Workers' comp pays medical care and part of lost wages but no pain and suffering; report injuries "promptly" (the 30-day rule was left out).
- Left out: expert-declaration requirements and §364 notice contents; MICRA attorney-fee limits and periodic payments; collateral-source rules; premises "knew or should have known" notice standard and open-and-obvious doctrine; design-defect tests; comp insurer's lien on third-party recoveries; what a government claim must contain and agency response time.
